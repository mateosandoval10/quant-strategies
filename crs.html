Channel Reversion System

CRS · NASDAQ:QQQ (primary) · daily · Mar 1999 – Aug 2026 · cross-validated on SPY
Published script on TradingView ↗

Thesis

Inside an established uptrend, pullbacks to the bottom of the recent trading range are statistically bought. CRS trades exactly that and nothing else: a 50-day Donchian channel maps the active range, the lower quarter is the accumulation zone, and entries only exist while price holds its 200-day SMA — mean reversion is traded with the prevailing trend, never against it. Fading downtrends is how reversion accounts die.

This is deliberately the low-octane piece of the library. It is flat roughly 80% of the time. The metric that matters is quality per unit of exposure, not raw beta — a 64% hit rate with a 15% worst drawdown while spending four-fifths of its life in cash is a capital-efficiency profile, and it survives on SPY with zero re-tuning.

The QQQ window now verified spans March 1999 to August 2026 — the full period includes the dot-com collapse and the 2008 crisis, and the worst drawdown across those 27 years is 15.2%. Stated plainly: CRS does not beat buy-and-hold on raw return — a system that is long 20% of the time cannot outrun a secular bull and this page will not pretend otherwise. What transfers across both symbols untouched is the character: high hit rate, symmetric small win/loss sizes, single-digit-to-teens drawdown. That is the shape you want in a sleeve that recycles cash into other systems the rest of the time.

Failed experiments (kept in the record)

REJECTED — "let winners ride" trail exit. Replacing the channel-midline profit target with a 2.5-ATR chandelier trail: same net return (+100%), profit factor down 2.28 → 1.79, win rate 64% → 47%, drawdown up 15% → 19%. In a mean-reversion system the target is the edge — the reversion to the middle of the range is the statistically reliable move; everything after it is noise you pay for.
REJECTED — leveraged wrapper (TQQQ). Running the identical signals on the 3x ETF: profit factor collapsed to 1.43 and max drawdown tripled to 43%. Triple leverage triples the noise around the structural stop and blows positions out of otherwise-valid reversion trades. Leverage belongs in systems designed for it (see VTTE), not bolted onto this one.

Technicals

Structure

Donchian(50) on prior-bar highs/lows (no same-bar self-reference). Entry zone = lower 25% of the channel. Target = channel midline.

Regime

Entries require close > SMA(200). A close below the SMA while positioned is an immediate regime exit — trend gone, thesis gone.

Risk

Hard stop one ATR(14) below the channel floor: if the range itself fails, the location thesis is falsified and the trade is wrong by definition.

Surface area

One entry condition, three exits, five inputs. Small tunable surface by design — fewer knobs, less curve to fit.

Source (core logic)

upperD  = ta.highest(high, chanLen)[1]
lowerD  = ta.lowest(low,  chanLen)[1]
midD    = (upperD + lowerD) / 2.0
zoneTop = lowerD + (upperD - lowerD) * zonePct / 100.0
trendOK = close > ta.sma(close, smaLen)

buySig = not inPos and trendOK and close <= zoneTop
if buySig
    strategy.entry("Long", strategy.long)

if inPos
    if close >= midD
        strategy.close("Long", comment="target: channel mid")
    else if close < lowerD - ta.atr(atrLen) * atrMult
        strategy.close("Long", comment="stop: channel failed")
    else if close < smaT
        strategy.close("Long", comment="exit: trend break")
Limitations. In-sample. The QQQ/SPY pair shares one market regime era; a genuine robustness claim needs non-US indices and a bear-market-heavy split. Planned: EFA/EEM cross-check and a 2000–2010 subperiod study, plus pairing analysis with VTTE (their exposure windows are largely complementary).
