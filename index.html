Research programs

The strategy library sits on top of a larger body of ongoing systematic work.

Live research · NQ futures

Intraday NQ program — paper record + 5-layer validation

A year-long research program on Nasdaq futures intraday systems (momentum + session-VWAP mean reversion). Every candidate passes five independent validation layers before deployment: Python backtest on exported bars, blind bar-replay, TradingView Strategy Tester cross-check, independent cloud reconstruction from a second data source, and a live paper record. Roughly 30 candidate improvements have been tested and rejected across loss autopsies, regime filters, circuit breakers and mechanics sweeps — including three separate incidents where an implausibly good result was traced to a data or fill-model artifact and killed. The surviving book runs unattended on a scheduled pipeline with automated health checks.

Discipline

The rejection log is the résumé

Findings that shaped everything on this site: faster reaction usually loses to slower rebalancing (turnover cost > crash protection); stops that look tight enough are usually load-bearing; filters fitted to a discovery set die on fresh data; a big backtest number carries almost no information about forward performance unless the tunable surface was small and the test was out-of-sample. Each strategy page carries its own REJECTED boxes with measured numbers — that log is the most defensible thing here.

TradingView research automation layer

An MCP (Model Context Protocol) toolchain driving TradingView Desktop over the Chrome DevTools Protocol: programmatic chart control, Pine Script compilation and deployment, Strategy Tester result extraction, bar-replay harvesting for historical intraday data, and multi-symbol batch runs. Every backtest cell on this site was produced through this pipeline.

Operations

Unattended execution & monitoring

Paper-trading bots and daily reconstruction jobs run on launchd schedules with a health-check agent that classifies failures (missed wake, environment regression, wrong-instrument guard, dead data feed) — built after real incidents, including one where a symbol-switch race put a session on the wrong instrument and range-checking the fill prices caught it.

What these results are not

Backtests are upper bounds on expectancy, not forecasts. The split-sample and slippage results reduce — but do not eliminate — selection effects; live paper trading is the next gate before any capital conclusion. Past performance does not predict future results. Nothing on this site is investment advice.
